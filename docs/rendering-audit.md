# How Ultima Offline 7 renders the original world

The native host owns each object's position, lift, frame, owner and game behavior. The renderer can change which pixels depict a door, but opening it must still select the original object and let Usecode determine its state. A native position is also the anchor for a moving object even when several positions share one ClassicUO tile. ### What the Trinsic fixes actually cover

| Fix | What decides the result | Scope and failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Walking, facing, picking and object edges | Project the four native positions within a 2 × 2 patch to distinct screen offsets. Retain native position for picking, interaction and movement. | Reusable part of the client. Test reversals, overlapping items, dragging, occlusion and floor changes together. A draw offset and a different pick offset recreate the original “only clickable at the feet” problem. |
| Fortress pavement and corners | Expand original fortress footprints; collect tops at their true lift. Read adjacent occupied cells and source crenellations to choose exposed faces, corner posts and parapets. Mask the paver where the stock wall cap covers it. | One rule covers the town perimeter, including western turns. Mask correctness depends on the selected wall's anchor and height. Other wall materials and stacked or partial footprints require examples. The four western landmark coordinates in the verifier are assertions; the generator finds the corners from neighbors. [Fortress assembly](../game/qualification/town_scale.py) · [Per-cell verification](../game/qualification/verify_town_facings.py) |
| Gates and stairs | The grille footprint and lift give a four-quadrant opening. Stair direction follows flat rows along a flight until the native walking surface rises or falls. | Gate pavement still recognizes six named-by-ID floor frames. Stair material uses frame parity and specific stone shapes. The stair search indexes surfaces by horizontal position, so overlapping flights at different lifts need a separate case. [Gate pavement](../game/qualification/town_atlas.py) · [Stair resolution](../game/qualification/town_facing.py) |
| Wall and fence orientation, windows, roofs | Adjacent source footprints resolve joins. Four stock wall faces have a fitted timber or stone window two native lifts above their bases. Roofs use a fixed footprint and stock roof tables. | Wall material and shutter shape selection use known families. Roof ridge direction and pitch are inferred from rectangular spans. A new material, irregular roof or window spanning several bases can look plausible while depicting the wrong structure. The compiler now leaves unmatched window and wall records on their ordinary rendering path. All 96 matched Trinsic window/shutter references use the four supported wall faces. |
| Door size and rotation | Stock door leaves have their original pixels. Native shape/frame and footprint select material, axis, leaf count and hinge. | A family-specific art substitution. The native engine still changes door state and determines passage. A new door family needs checks through a full open/close cycle, including clicks on the visible panel and crossing its threshold. [Door leaves](../game/qualification/town_scale.py) |
| Green floor and 4-to-1 compression | Four source materials can share one land diamond. Color masks distinguish grass, soil and stone. The compact cell takes the highest of its four source elevations. | The projection is reusable; the material classifier and single height are assumptions. A slope, shoreline or ledge can look flat or elevated underfoot while native movement remains correct. Outside the town bounds the landscape pass includes neighbor and generic ground fallbacks. [Terrain conversion](../game/qualification/town_scale.py) |
| Keys, pitchforks, signs and the approved rake | Shape/frame recipes select, combine or transform stock pixels. Carried and world art can differ. | Shared within their mapped families. The larger key applies to shape 641; the rake approval covers ten frames of shape 620. A successful substitution establishes neither the scale of every small object nor its gameplay behavior. [Facing and tool art](../game/qualification/town_facing.py) · [Rake recipe](../game/atlas/complete-town/rake.py) |
| Stable victim | The renderer matches `(shape 867, frame 0, x 1072, y 2199, lift 0)`. It turns the sprite 45 degrees clockwise and adds a one-diamond vertical offset. | Deliberate scene exception. The native body stays put. Another body with the same graphic keeps its normal pose. This is the clearest example of a fix to keep local. [Placement override](../game/qualification/town_atlas.py) |

### What we measured

For every timber or stone house, a wall face ends at the compact vertex where the original run crosses a tile boundary at the same lift. When both incoming faces use one material, one corner sprite carries both. At a timber-to-stone junction each face keeps its material. Where both walls run away from a vertex, a post closes the seam. Doorways keep their native openings and a fitted window replaces only its own face. [House wall conversion](../game/qualification/house_walls.py)

The compact overlay verifier reads the generated art and static records. It checks 54,427 Trinsic placements, all 894 house-wall cells, 49 stair rows, 72 connected fence posts, 2,312 compact roof cells and 28 approved art treatments. The wall check independently traces native wall and doorway footprints, then counts the faces and posts present in the installed facet at each height. [House wall verification](../game/qualification/verify_house_walls.py)

The installed Forge world contains 9,437,184 terrain placements using 5,600 distinct shape/frame pairs. The reviewed Trinsic catalog lacks an exact row for 3,749 of those pairs, accounting for **2,865,369 placements**. Historical compiler rules or fallback art can cover some of them. This measurement tells us where the Trinsic atlas supplies no exact mapping. Run `py -3 -B game/atlas/complete-town/verify_town.py --world-terrain` to repeat it. [Atlas verifier](../game/atlas/complete-town/verify_town.py)

### How to qualify the next region

1. Render a second settlement using the same converter and original records. Include a turn, a gated opening, stacked floors and a building crossing a region boundary. Include adjacent-region records when deciding whether an edge is exposed.
2. For a failing object, identify the original file and offset, shape/frame, footprint, lift, selected art and rule. Classify the correction by what actually determines the answer: coordinate transform, stock family, neighboring geometry or one placed narrative object.
3. Check the new region and Trinsic together. Compare emitted geometry and pixels, then walk, select, operate and reload representative objects in ClassicUO. Protect native identity, inventory and walkability independently of the art. Record any unresolved placement or fallback before calling a rule world-ready.
